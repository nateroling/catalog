The Catalog is a collection of “things” that I want to share with The Internet.

It’s inspired in part by [Martin Fowler’s Bliki](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/WhatIsaBliki.html), which I find endlessly useful as a reference point when discussing or researching topics. The ability to point to an entry on a topic to say "here, this is what I mean" is incredibly useful, and I'm not sure anyone's done it better than Martin Fowler. His bliki certainly isn't comprehensive, and the depth of the articles varies dramatically, but it's **useful, **and I'm hoping this will be as well, if only for myself.

Some examples from Fowler’s Bliki:

- [https://martinfowler.com/articles/continuousIntegration.html](https://martinfowler.com/articles/continuousIntegration.html)
- [https://martinfowler.com/books/refactoring.html](https://martinfowler.com/books/refactoring.html)
- [https://martinfowler.com/bliki/VibeCoding.html](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/VibeCoding.html)

It’s also influenced by [A Pattern Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Pattern_Language), which also influenced [Design Patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_Patterns), which popularized the idea of software patterns in general.

Yes, I own a copy of A Pattern Lanugage. No, I haven’t read it all. The bits I **have** read are really interseting though! My copy is 1,171 pages of bible-thin paper. As I was opening it to check the page count, I opened to page 1,105, which is “Filtered Light”:

> tk read and find a quote

*A Pattern Language* works through patterns of designing spaces from large to small scale, and each pattern ends with a summarized "rule”. The first pattern, "Independent Regions”, ends like this:

> Whenever possible, work toward the evolution of independent regions in the world; each with a population between 2 and 10 million; each with its own natural and geographic boundaries; each with its own economy; each one autonomous and self-governing; each with a seat in a world government, without the intervening power of larger states and countries.

Around page 750, “Light on Two Sides of Every Room” encourages

> Locate each room so that it has outdoor space outside it on at least two sides, and then place windows in these outdoor walls so that natural light falls into every from from more than one direction.

The final pattern, “Things From Your Life”, discusses decorating a room, and ends with

> Do not be tricked into believing that modern decor must be slick or psychedilic, or “natural” or “modern art,” or “plants” or anything else that current taste-makers claim. It is most beautiful when it comes straight from your life—the things you care for, the things that tell your story.
